# Fictional selected-text workflow examples

These are authored illustrations, not records of host execution or verified candidate facts.
Each input assumes the host disclosed provider processing and the user explicitly agreed to
share the selected fictional text in that conversation. Denial stops analysis. Later sessions
require a fresh agreement and deliberate selection again. No file or core state is created.

## Setup

Input: "Nora leads five support specialists. She wants more coaching responsibility and less
reactive ticket work. She needs to stay in Cologne, can travel two days a month, and prefers
remote work. Her pay threshold is not decided. Build a brief and next-step plan."

Output:

- Starting point: leadership of five support specialists; scope beyond that is not supplied.
- Direction: more coaching and planned improvement work, less reactive queue coverage.
- Firm constraints: remain in Cologne; travel at most two days monthly.
- Preference: remote work. Required office frequency and pay threshold remain open.
- Role directions: support-team leadership with a coaching mandate is the closest supplied
  fit. Customer-operations leadership may add process scope, but broader ownership is unproven.
- Next: list two examples of coaching/improvement with personal actions and outcomes, then
  assess one pasted posting. Decide acceptable office frequency and minimum fixed pay before
  comparing offers. No vacancy has been researched or application prepared.

## Role

Input: "Posting: Service Operations Lead. Must coach six specialists, own monthly reporting,
hold a completed bachelor's degree and attend Cologne office three days weekly. Nora leads
five specialists and runs weekly coaching. She completed some degree coursework, not a
degree. Monthly reporting experience isn't supplied. She needs remote work. Assess fit."

Output: Clarify the degree and office requirements before investing in application work.
Coaching is directly supported at a similar team scale; six-person scope is transferable,
not evidence Nora already led six. Monthly reporting is unknown. A completed degree is a
genuine gap. Three office days conflict with the stated remote constraint. Broader decision
rights, pay and reporting line are unknown, so the title does not establish progression.
Ask whether equivalent experience satisfies the degree requirement, whether office frequency
is flexible, and what reporting/operational authority the role owns. No application is drafted.

## Application

Input: "Posting: Support Lead, coach a team and improve repeat-contact handling. Write a
60-word maximum answer to 'What would you bring?' only. Use Note A and say I completed
coursework in business studies without a degree. Do not use the disputed team count."

Output brief: Emphasize weekly coaching and shared process improvement. Omit disputed
headcount. Retain the approximate team result. The degree is not required in this supplied
posting and need not be inserted into this answer; it must remain incomplete if later used.

Complete answer (41 words):

"I bring hands-on team coaching and experience improving recurring support issues. In 2024,
I ran weekly case coaching, while our team helped reduce repeat contacts by about 9%. I would
bring that practical focus to developing specialists and improving day-to-day support."

No letter, CV, file or submission is produced.

## Interview

Input: "General practice for that Support Lead role; no interview is scheduled. Nora reviewed
repeat cases with colleagues, introduced weekly case coaching and collected common questions.
The team helped reduce repeat contacts by about 9% in 2024. Prepare an opening and story."

Output opening: "I lead a support team and spend time helping specialists work through recurring
cases. I'm interested in a role where coaching and practical service improvement are a larger
part of the mandate. This role seems worth discussing on that basis."

Grounded answer: "We were seeing repeated contacts on familiar issues. I reviewed cases with
colleagues, introduced weekly coaching and collected the common questions so we could address
them together. In 2024, our team helped reduce repeat contacts by about 9%. That was a shared
result; I can explain my part in the coaching and case-review work."

Follow-up prompt: clarify the metric's baseline and measurement period before using a more
detailed numerical story. Do not claim sole causation.

Call sheet: Objective—test the coaching mandate. Opening cue—support leadership and weekly
coaching. Story cues—case review, common questions, shared approximate 9%. Questions—how much
time is protected for coaching; who owns process changes; how is success measured? Logistics
and panel are unknown; scheduling is not asserted.

## Outcomes

Input: "For September: two applications explicitly submitted, one recruiter screen completed,
one employer rejection saying the role was cancelled. A third application is only a draft.
The other submitted role remains pending. I felt rushed in the screen. Review and plan."

Output: Two supplied submissions, one completed screen and one employer-stated cancellation.
The third draft is excluded from submission counts. The pending role has no inferred outcome.
The sample is too small and associations/timing are incomplete for a reliable conversion trend.

Observation: one stated cancellation is an employer-process result, not evidence of a skill
gap. Hypothesis: the rushed screen may reflect answer pacing; it does not establish why an
employer decided. Next steps: rehearse the opening and one grounded story aloud, confirm the
pending role's stated next-contact timing before deciding on a follow-up, and review the third
draft only if requested. No follow-up is sent and no pipeline or campaign event is saved.
